01 / The gap

One class is all
you get.

Turbine monitoring data is not merely imbalanced. It is 99.53% normal, and the abnormal windows sit inside the normal cloud. CSiamese is built for that world: train on health, then ask how well health can reconstruct what it sees.

7,593normal windows in the dataset
36abnormal windows in the dataset
1 in 212windows is abnormal — a prevalence of 0.47%

The paper collects real cruise data from 36 gas turbines at an Asia-based airline. Three engine parameters — Delta Exhaust Gas Temperature, Delta Fuel Flow and Delta Core Speed — are cut into 10 × 3 sliding windows with a step of five cycles.

Two failure modes follow. A supervised model sees a class imbalance and learns to favour “normal.” And because abnormal windows are widely scattered, a new fault type may not resemble anything in the training labels at all.

02 / Method

Reconstruct.
Compare. Decide.

Three moves replace a supervised classifier. A convolutional auto-encoder learns what normal looks like, a Siamese network learns how to measure the distance between an input and its reconstruction, and a maximum-likelihood threshold turns that distance into a decision.

01

Reconstruct the normal

A CAE is trained only on normal windows. It learns an encoder–decoder mapping whose L1 reconstruction error is minimised for health, so an abnormal component has little influence on the gradient.

Conv 4 × 4L1 losslatent 128
02

Compare in latent space

The input and its reconstruction go through a shared-weight Siamese encoder. Their Euclidean distance becomes a reconstruction-similarity score — a learned metric that is less brittle than a raw pixel error.

shared weightspositive pairsnoise-aware
03

Decide with a threshold

Normal training scores are fitted with an Alpha distribution by maximum likelihood. The upper end of the 90% confidence interval becomes β: below it, normal; at or above it, abnormal.

MLETSβ = 0.127090% CI

Inside the CAE

Compression is the prior.

The encoder is a stack of convolution, batch normalisation and LeakyReLU layers; the decoder reverses it with deconvolutions, ReLU and a final tanh. The architecture follows the DCGAN pattern, and the paper uses an L1 norm because it produces a less blurry reconstruction than L2.

The whole system

A reconstruction pipeline with a learned judge.

The CAE is trained first, using only normal samples. Its reconstruction of a window is paired with the original input — a positive pair by construction. The Siamese network is then trained on those pairs, with weights transferred from the trained encoder.

At test time, the score is the distance between the two embeddings. No negative pairs are needed to train the similarity metric; the threshold handles the decision.

Why triangle loss

Positive pairs can teach a network to say nothing.

With only positive pairs, contrastive loss has a trivial minimum: map everything to a constant, and the distance is zero. The triangle loss adds the path through the encoder’s latent anchor h, creating an upper bound that cannot be minimised by collapse.

d(FW(x), h)+d(h, FW(x̂))≥d(FW(x), FW(x̂))

03 / Evidence

The gain is small.
The ordering is not.

On the held-out gas-turbine set, CSiamese reaches 97.53% AUROC. The margin over a CAE is 0.91 points — modest in isolation, but consistent across the comparison and paired with a usable decision threshold.

AUROC is threshold-free: 50% is chance, 100% is a perfect ranking. The confusion matrix uses the learned β, so it reflects an operating point rather than a best-case sweep.

04 / Generalization

A second domain.
Same direction.

CIFAR-10 is the paper’s stress test outside gas turbines: one class is held out as abnormal, the other nine are normal. CSiamese wins 7 of 10 held-out classes and keeps its lead as noise increases.

Beyond turbines

The same score travels.

No architecture is changed for the image experiment. One class is treated as abnormal and the rest as normal, with no abnormal examples in training. The learned similarity still separates the held-out class better than a raw CAE reconstruction error on seven of ten labels.

The exceptions are informative: a car can reconstruct well from a model trained on ships and trucks, so its anomaly score stays low. Similarity is only as good as the normal manifold it has seen.

Robustness

The lead survives the noise.

Additive white Gaussian noise is injected at four variances. At the strongest setting (1.0), CSiamese averages 75.02% AUROC; the CAE falls to 56.02% and the DAE to 52.02%. The Siamese branch pulls the noisy input and its reconstruction together instead of asking a raw distance to do all the work.

05 / The boundary

When similarity lies.

The car class is the honest failure case. A car is visually close to ships and trucks, so a CAE trained on those normal classes reconstructs it well — and the Siamese network further pulls the input toward that reconstruction. The anomaly score stays low, and the detector misses it.

The paper’s own conclusion: future work should model the diversity of normal components explicitly and weaken the representation capacity of abnormal components. Similarity is a useful prior, not a universal boundary.

86.82%CAE · car as abnormal class
67.97%CSiamese · car as abnormal class

One failure mode, stated plainly. The average still rises; the tail matters.

06 / Reproduce

The recipe,
in six settings.

Everything needed to read the method at a glance. The full architecture tables and hyperparameter sweeps live in Appendices A and B of the paper.

10 × 3sliding window

Three sensor channels, ten cycles per window, moved five cycles at a time.

128latent dimension

The CAE and Siamese encoder share the same latent width.

64batch size

Adam, initial learning rate 0.0002, β₁ = 0.5, β₂ = 0.999.

4 / 6training epochs

Four epochs for the CAE, then six for the Siamese network.

0.1270decision threshold

Upper limit of the 90% confidence interval from MLETS.

0.01–1.0noise variance

Four AWGN settings used in the CIFAR-10 robustness study.
